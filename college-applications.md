# The College Application Keeper

*For a student doing this without a counselor, without a parent who has done it before, and without money for either.*

A private college counselor costs thousands of dollars. Most of what they do is not insight — it is **bookkeeping**: knowing what is due when, what can be waived, who to ask and how early, and keeping a running record of what you have actually done so it is there when the application asks. That part can be given away, and this is it.

The premise is simple and it is the whole thing:

> **Students don't lose places because they lack accomplishments. They lose them because nobody kept a record, and by the time the application asks, the evidence is gone.**

You have been doing things for years. Working. Watching your brother. Translating for your mother at the doctor. Nobody wrote any of it down, and in October you will sit in front of a blank box and think you have nothing to say. **You have plenty. It was just never recorded.**

---

## What this will not do

- **It will not tell you your chances.** Nobody can, the people who claim to are guessing, and a number would only make you feel worse.
- **It will not write your essay.** It interviews you and hands your own words back organised. An essay that does not sound like you is worse than a plain one that does.
- **It will not tell you what a school costs.** It tells you where to look and what to ask, because the sticker price is almost never the real price and only the school's aid office knows yours.

---

## The four files

| | |
|---|---|
| `ME.md` | Everything you have done, logged as it happens. The evidence bank. |
| `LIST.md` | Schools, deadlines, what each one requires, what each one costs to apply to. |
| `MONEY.md` | Fee waivers, aid forms, scholarships, and every deadline attached to them. |
| `PEOPLE.md` | Who is writing for you, when you asked, and what you gave them. |

⚠ **`ME.md` is the one that matters and the one everyone skips.** Add to it every week, in two lines, even when nothing feels notable. In October it is the difference between an application and a blank page.

---

## The prompt

```
I'm applying to college and I'm doing it on my own. Help me keep track of it.
Create four files — ME.md, LIST.md, MONEY.md, PEOPLE.md — and then work through
the steps below with me. One question at a time. Don't give me a wall of text.

ABSOLUTE RULES

1. Never tell me my chances of getting in. Not a number, not a word like
   'reach' used as a verdict. You don't know and neither does anyone else.

2. Never write my essay for me. Interview me, then give my own sentences back
   to me in an order that works. If a line doesn't sound like something I'd
   say out loud, cut it.

3. Verify every deadline, every fee, and every requirement against the
   school's own website FOR THIS YEAR before you tell it to me. Never from
   memory. Dates move every year. If you can't check it, say so and put it on
   my list to confirm.

4. Never assume I have anything. Not a parent who can help, not a counselor,
   not a car, not a printer, not a quiet room, not money for a fee. If a step
   needs one of those, say so up front and give me the version that doesn't.

5. Every question I ask goes on my list, even if you answer it. Money and
   financial aid questions get answered AND go on the list, because the only
   real answer comes from that school's financial aid office.

6. I can stop at any time. See BANKING.

BANKING — offer this at every stage, in these words

  'If you want to stop, say BANK THIS and I'll save everything and tell you
   exactly where we left off.'

When I say BANK THIS:
  - Save everything to the files. Nothing is lost.
  - Tell me in three lines: what we have, what's next, and nothing else.
  - Do not ask if I'm sure. Do not ask why. Do not talk me into continuing.
  - Do not make me re-explain anything when I come back.
  - ONE EXCEPTION: if a deadline is inside two weeks, say it once, plainly,
    on the way out. The break is free. The deadline isn't.

STEP 1 — ME.md, and start here

Ask me what I actually do in a week. Do NOT use the word 'extracurriculars' —
that word makes people say 'nothing.' Ask instead:

  - Do you have a job? How many hours? Since when?
  - Do you take care of anyone — a sibling, a grandparent, a parent?
  - Do you translate or interpret for your family?
  - Do you cook, drive people, handle bills, make appointments?
  - Is there anything you do every week that nobody asked you to do?
  - What are you good at that no one has ever given you credit for?

ALL OF THAT COUNTS. Twenty hours a week at a job is not less impressive than
twenty hours of debate club; it is more. Log each one with what I do, how
often, and since when. Keep adding every week.

STEP 2 — MONEY.md, and do it BEFORE the essays

Application fees stop more people than essays do, and almost every fee can be
waived. Walk me through what waivers exist for my situation and how to get
each one, matter-of-factly — this is a normal thing that a very large number
of applicants use, not a favour anyone is doing me.

Then find every financial aid form I have to file, the date each one opens and
closes THIS year, and any earlier 'priority' deadline for aid or scholarships
— those are usually before the application deadline and missing one costs more
money than anything else on this list.

STEP 3 — LIST.md

Build the school list with me, and for each one make me say WHY in one
sentence that isn't about ranking. For each school record: deadline and which
kind (early, regular, rolling); application fee and whether my waiver covers
it; whether test scores are required, optional, or not considered this year;
what essays it wants; and what its aid deadline is.

STEP 4 — PEOPLE.md

Recommendation letters. Tell me who to ask, and help me ask at least a month
before the deadline. Then help me write them a one-page note about myself —
what I did in their class, what I'm proud of, what I want them to mention.
Teachers write better letters when they're given material, and asking for that
is normal, not presumptuous. Record who I asked, when, what I gave them, and
when to send a polite reminder.

STEP 5 — every week

Ask me three questions: what happened this week, what's due in the next two
weeks, and what am I stuck on. Update the files. Show me the next three things
in order.
```

## The rules that carry the weight

**Count the invisible work.** A student who works thirty hours a week and raises a sibling has done more than a student with six clubs, and the application will not tell them that. The most useful thing this does is refuse to let that go unwritten.

**Verify every date this year, never from memory.** Deadlines, fee amounts, testing policies and aid-form open dates all move. A confidently remembered date is the single most expensive kind of error here, because a missed aid deadline cannot be appealed later.

**Money before essays.** Fee waivers and aid deadlines decide more outcomes than prose does, and they come earlier. Anything that surfaces them last has the order backwards.

**Never require an adult.** No step may assume a parent who can help, a counselor who returns email, a ride, a printer, or a quiet room. If a step needs one, it has to also offer the version that doesn't. **A tool that quietly assumes you have someone has already excluded the person who needs it most.**

**You can put it down.** Applications get abandoned in the middle far more often than they get rejected. Say BANK THIS and everything is saved, with no lecture and nothing to re-explain when you come back. **The break is free.** Only a deadline inside two weeks follows you out the door, said once.

**No chances, ever.** Predicting admission is guessing dressed as expertise. It changes nothing a student can control and it talks people out of applying.

---

## Why the record is the whole trick

Everything above is bookkeeping, and bookkeeping is what money buys in this process. A counselor's real advantage is not knowing a secret — it is that somebody has been writing things down since ninth grade.

**Two lines a week, starting whenever you start, beats a perfect memory in October.** That is the entire method, and it is free.
