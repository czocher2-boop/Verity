# Reporting Harassment at Work

*Building the record — before, during, and after you report.*

This does not decide anything for you. It interviews you, keeps your words exactly as you said them, and produces the two documents that matter: **a dated log** you keep adding to, and **a report** you can hand to HR, an agency, or a lawyer.

**The report is a moment. The log is the case.** Most people write the report and stop. The log is what survives.

---

## Four things to know before you start

**HR works for the employer.** That isn't cynicism, it's the org chart. A report is a business record, it can be produced in litigation, and the company's interests activate the moment you file. Knowing that before you decide is different from finding it out after.

**There is a clock, and it is shorter than anyone expects.** The federal deadline to file a charge with the EEOC is commonly **180 days** from the conduct, extended to **300 days** in states with their own enforcement agency. State deadlines can be shorter. ⚠ **Miss it and the claim is gone regardless of what happened to you.** Confirm your own deadline with the EEOC or your state agency — this week, not eventually.

**Never take the company's confidential documents.** The instinct is overwhelming and it has destroyed cases and gotten people fired. Preserve what is *yours*: your own texts, your own notes, your own calendar, emails you send to your personal account. Keep copies somewhere your employer cannot reach.

**Write down who you told, and when.** The person you called that night is evidence. Contemporaneous accounts to friends, family, a doctor, or a therapist corroborate a record in a way nothing else does, and almost nobody thinks to note them.

## The prompt

```
Help me build a record of what is happening to me at work and, if I decide to,
a report I can file. Work through this with me one question at a time.

ABSOLUTE RULES

1. You do not tell me whether to report. That is a decision with costs only I
   can weigh. Lay out what each path involves and stop there.

2. You do not tell me whether what happened is illegal, actionable, or 'severe
   enough.' That's a lawyer's call. You may tell me what a lawyer or an agency
   will want to see, and put the question on my list.

3. MY WORDS, EXACTLY. Record what I say verbatim. Never smooth it, never
   soften it, never upgrade it into legal language. If you infer anything —
   a date, a sequence, what someone meant — label it as YOUR inference,
   separately, and leave my words untouched.

4. Never invent a date. If I don't remember, ask once whether anything anchors
   it — a payday, a holiday, a trip, a shift change. If that doesn't work,
   write down what I said and mark the date unconfirmed. Do not guess.

5. I can stop at any time. See BANKING, below.

6. NEVER CONFUSE 'I DON'T REMEMBER' WITH 'I HAVE NO PROOF.' They sound
   identical when I say them and they are opposite facts. See THE FOUR STATES.

BANKING — offer this at every stage, in these words

  'If you want to stop, say BANK THIS and I'll save everything and tell you
   exactly where we left off.'

When I say BANK THIS:
  - Save everything captured so far to the log. Nothing is lost.
  - Tell me in three lines: what we have, what's next, and nothing else.
  - Do not ask if I'm sure. Do not ask why. Do not encourage me to continue.
  - Do not make me re-explain anything when I come back.
  - ONE EXCEPTION: if a filing deadline is running, say it once, plainly,
    on the way out. The break is free. The clock is not, and it is the only
    thing that can't wait for me.

STEP 1 — SAFETY

Ask first whether I'm physically safe, and whether anyone has threatened me or
followed me. If yes, say plainly that this is beyond an HR matter and stop
collecting until I've said what I want to do.

STEP 2 — THE LOG

For each incident, take: the date and time; where; who was present; what was
said or done, IN MY WORDS; what I did; who I told and when; and what it changed
about my work — shifts I dropped, meetings I avoided, projects I lost, days I
missed.

Go through every incident I want to include. Do not stop early to spare me and
do not ask whether I want to skip one. I am already living this; the record is
the point. But if I say BANK THIS, bank it immediately.

Then ask what I haven't mentioned: earlier incidents I dismissed at the time,
things that happened to other people that I saw, and anything that started
before I noticed a pattern.

THE FOUR STATES — run this for every fact, every time

When I say 'I'm not sure' or 'I can't prove that,' find out which of these I
actually mean. Ask plainly: 'Do you not remember it — or do you remember it
and there's just no paper?'

  1. I REMEMBER IT AND I HAVE SOMETHING.
     -> Log it, note what the something is, and tell me to preserve a copy off
     company systems.

  2. I REMEMBER IT CLEARLY AND THERE IS NO DOCUMENT AND THERE NEVER WAS.
     -> This is TESTIMONY, AND TESTIMONY IS EVIDENCE. Most of what happens to
     people happens out loud, in a hallway, with nobody taking minutes. Record
     it at FULL STRENGTH, in my words, with the date. Do NOT mark it weak, do
     NOT mark it unconfirmed, and do NOT write 'no supporting documentation'
     as though that were a finding. Instead ask who I told and when — that is
     the corroboration this kind of fact takes.

  3. IT EXISTED AND I CAN'T GET IT NOW — deleted, on a work laptop I returned,
     in an account they disabled, in a chat that auto-purges.
     -> A DIFFERENT fact, and a more serious one. Record what it was, where it
     lived, when I last saw it, and how I lost access. Then tell me to stop
     deleting anything of my own from here on.

  4. I GENUINELY DON'T REMEMBER.
     -> A memory gap, not a proof problem. Probe once for an anchor, then mark
     it unconfirmed and move on. Never let a gap harden into a date.

⚠ STATES 2 AND 4 ARE THE ONES THAT GET COLLAPSED, AND ALWAYS IN THE SAME
DIRECTION: someone who remembers perfectly says 'I can't prove it,' and the
record writes her down as uncertain. That is the most damaging thing this tool
could do. Split them every time.

STEP 3 — WHAT I ALREADY HAVE

Walk me through what exists: texts, emails, chats, photos, calendar entries,
my own notes, performance reviews before and after, schedules.

Tell me to preserve copies somewhere my employer cannot reach — a personal
account or device. Tell me plainly NOT to take confidential company documents.
Screenshots of things sent TO me are mine; the company's files are not.

STEP 4 — WHAT I'M ASKING FOR

Ask what outcome I actually want: it to stop, a schedule change, a different
reporting line, a transfer that isn't mine to take, an apology, an
investigation, money, or to leave with something. Most reports never say, and
a report that names the remedy is a different document from one that only
names the harm.

STEP 5 — THE REPORT

Draft it: what happened, in order, with dates; who witnessed it; what I already
reported and to whom and what came of it; the effect on my work; and what I'm
asking for. Factual, chronological, unembroidered. No adjectives doing work
that facts should do. Anything unconfirmed is marked unconfirmed — and nothing
in state 2 is marked unconfirmed.

Tell me what my options are for filing — internal HR, an anonymous or
third-party hotline if my employer has one, a state agency, the EEOC, a
lawyer — and note that many employment lawyers consult for free and take cases
on contingency. Do not tell me which to pick.

STEP 6 — AFTER

THE LOG DOES NOT STOP WHEN I FILE. Keep prompting me to record what happens
next: schedule changes, exclusions, a sudden review, reassignment, a manager
who stops speaking to me. Retaliation is often easier to prove than the
underlying conduct and it has its own clock. Tell me that once, now.
```

## "I don't remember" and "I have no proof" are opposite facts

They come out of a person's mouth as the same sentence — *I'm not sure I can prove that* — and they are not remotely the same thing.

**A clear memory with no document is testimony, and testimony is evidence.** Harassment happens out loud, in hallways, in cars, with nobody taking minutes. A record that files a vivid, dated, specific account under *unsupported* has quietly adopted the other side's theory of the case. The corroboration for that kind of fact isn't paper — it's **who you told, and when.**

⚠ The collapse only ever runs one direction. Nobody upgrades a memory gap into a certainty; people downgrade certainty into doubt, constantly, and about themselves. **The tool's job is to refuse that downgrade on your behalf.**

---

## Why banking is in every step

A tool that requires you to finish in one sitting is a tool for someone whose life is calm. **The break has to be free** — no justification, no are-you-sure, no starting over when you come back. The state lives in the file, not in your memory, so putting it down costs you nothing.

⚠ **With one exception, and it is the only paternalism in here.** If a filing deadline is running, it gets said once on the way out. Everything else can wait for you. That can't.

---

## The rules that carry the weight

**Your words, exactly.** A record in your own language is more durable than one translated into legal vocabulary, and an inference written down as a fact is a hole someone else will find later.

**The log outlives the report.** Contemporaneous, dated, boring, and continuous. It is worth more than any single well-written document, and it is the part only you can make.

**Who you told is evidence.** Note every person and every date.

**Never assess, always name.** Whether it was 'severe or pervasive' is a legal question. Naming the question and putting it on your list is preparation. Answering it is advice, and advice from a machine is worth what you paid.
